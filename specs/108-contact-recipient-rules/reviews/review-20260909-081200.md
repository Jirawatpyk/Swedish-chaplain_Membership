## The result, stated once

**Round 1's fixes introduced five new defects. Three of them write falsehoods into
an append-only table or into a member's inbox — outcomes strictly worse than the
bugs they replaced.**

The most serious is on the leg that is **live at merge** (flag OFF), not on the leg
the flag protects. Round 2 was not a formality on this branch, and the reason it
was not is the same reason round 1 wasn't: on 108, a fix's blind spot is where the
next finding lives.

Round 1's diagnosis — "the new sibling dropped what the old path accumulated" — was
correct and the four dropped things were genuinely restored. What round 2 found is
that restoring them introduced a *different* class: machinery that now records a
confident, wrong answer where it previously recorded nothing.

### One finding is half-closed on purpose

**R2-21** asked for the terminal provider-status set to be INVERTED — allowlist

Resend's import-status vocabulary is **unverified**: `research.md` § R9 and
`contracts/broadcast-audience.md` document only `completed`; `failed`/`canceled`
were inferred. An allowlist built on a guess turns a genuinely in-flight status
into an immediate terminal failure — **a lost send** — whereas the defect it fixes
is a wrong cause recorded 30 minutes later, with the send already gone. That
asymmetry decides it. `'unknown'` IS now terminal: it is not a provider status at
all, it is the gateway's own sentinel for a response with no `status` field, so
treating it as in-flight means polling a reply we could not read.
